## Mooring configurations — physical description

There are three mooring types in this dataset. Understanding their geometry is critical for
the standing-wave bias analysis in §E.

### above_50

The mooring line is attached **above** the waterline, at 50 mm above the water surface.
Line length is approximately **7–9 cm** — very short and taut. This keeps the panel close
to the mooring attachment point with very little slack. Because the line is short and above
the water, the panel is held in a stiff, nearly fixed position relative to the tank wall.

**Consequence for geometry**: with a 7–9 cm line attached above the water on the upstream
(paddle) side, the panel leading edge ends up only **≈5 cm** from the IN probe at 9373/170.
The panel barely moves longitudinally.

### below_90_loose230 and below_90_loose300

The mooring line is attached **below** the waterline, at 90 mm below the water surface.
Line lengths are **230 mm** and **300 mm** respectively (the two variants represent different
rubber band lengths used across experimental sessions — the difference is small and the two
have been shown to be statistically indistinguishable for OUT/IN).

With a 23–30 cm line attached below water, the panel can swing further upstream (toward the
paddle) when wave forces push it. The panel leading edge sits approximately **20–25 cm** from
the IN probe at 9373/170 — significantly further than above_50.

### Physical interpretation of the difference

The above-water mooring acts as a rigid tether that pulls the panel back upstream when
waves push it forward. Very short line = very stiff = panel stays close to probe.

The below-water mooring is submerged rubber — it is elastic and allows more longitudinal
movement. With 23–30 cm of line, the panel has room to equilibrate further from the probe.

This geometry difference — 5 cm vs 25 cm between panel leading edge and IN probe — is the
root cause of the standing-wave measurement bias discussed in §E.

## A. Standing-wave correction test (`sw_correction.py`)

### Motivation

The standing wave between paddle and panel (caused by panel reflection) modulates the IN
probe amplitude. If R=0.20, the node/antinode pattern would create a ±15% zigzag in the
raw OUT/IN curve. The question was: should we apply a correction to remove this bias before
reporting OUT/IN?

### Method

Compute the standing-wave amplitude factor
  SW_factor(f) = sqrt(1 + R² + 2R·cos(2kΔ))
with R=0.20, panel at x=11.0 m, IN probe at x=9.373 m (Δ=1.627 m). Apply
  T_corrected = OUT/IN_obs × SW_factor.
Test whether the corrected curve is smoother than the raw curve (smoothness metric:
sum of squared second differences across frequencies).

### Result: STRONGLY NEGATIVE

- Correction at R=0.20 makes the curve 4350% (nowind) and 5963% (fullwind) LESS smooth.
- Sensitivity grid of 30 (R, panel_position) combinations: 0/30 cases smoother for nowind.
- The raw OUT/IN is already smooth — the data contain NO detectable node/antinode pattern
  at the frequency spacing implied by R=0.20.

### Conclusion

R < ~0.05 based on the smoothness argument. No correction needed. The raw OUT/IN curve
should be reported directly.

## B. Mansard-Funke two-probe decomposition (`mansard_funke.py`)

### Motivation

With R < 0.05 as an upper bound from the smoothness test, the next step was a direct
measurement of R via the two-probe Mansard-Funke method using the 8804/250 and 9373/170
probes (probe separation Δ = 0.569 m).

### Critical bug: Fourier sign convention (this took significant time to diagnose)

The original code produced R values of 10–150. The root cause:

**Python `np.fft.fft` uses the engineering DFT convention.** For a rightward wave
η = A·cos(kx − ωt), the positive-frequency FFT bin gives z ∝ A·exp(−ikx), not exp(+ikx).

The textbook Mansard-Funke formula assumes the physics convention (exp(+ikx) for the
incident wave). Using the wrong convention swaps A and B in the linear system, so:
- A_code = B_true (the reflected amplitude)
- B_code = A_true (the incident amplitude)
- R_code = |B_code/A_code| = |A_true/B_true| = 1/R_true → R≈10–150 when R_true≈0.05–0.10

**The diagnostic that confirmed this**: measure arg(z₂/z₁) for a pure rightward wave.
Expected: −kΔ (mod 2π) = −kΔ + 2π when −kΔ < −π. At 1.4 Hz:
  - k = 7.889 rad/m, Δ = 0.569 m
  - Expected: −7.889 × 0.569 + 2π = +1.794 rad
  - Measured: 1.688 rad → excellent agreement. Confirms rightward wave with exp(−ikx) convention.

**Fixed formulas (Python-adapted Mansard-Funke):**

The model to solve is:
  z₁ = A·exp(−ikx₁) + B·exp(+ikx₁)
  z₂ = A·exp(−ikx₂) + B·exp(+ikx₂)

Solution:
  A_inc = (z₁·exp(+ikx₂) − z₂·exp(+ikx₁)) / (2i·sin(kΔ))
  B_ref = (z₂·exp(−ikx₁) − z₁·exp(−ikx₂)) / (2i·sin(kΔ))

Synthetic verification: R=0 for pure incident (exact), R=0.0500 for R=0.05 input (exact).

### Results (nowind, 0.2V, |sin(kΔ)| > 0.20, quality_flag==ok)

| Mooring | n | median R | range |
|---------|---|----------|-------|
| above_50 | 17 | 0.058 | 0.03–0.13 |
| below_90_loose230 | 24 | 0.052 | 0.03–0.11 |
| below_90_loose300 | 9 | 0.057 | 0.01–0.14 |

- 1.2 Hz excluded: |sin(kΔ)| = 0.163 (ill-conditioned at this probe separation)
- 1.7 Hz fullwind R≈0.94 is an artifact — wind-wave phase contamination of IN probe
- Median R is consistent across all three mooring types → reflection is a panel property,
  not a mooring property (as expected physically; the panel geometry and material are the
  same regardless of how the panel is moored)

### Conclusion

R ≈ 0.05–0.07 (all moorings, nowind). Three independent estimates all agree:
1. Smoothness test: R < 0.05 (conservative upper bound)
2. Mansard-Funke: R ≈ 0.052–0.058 (median)
3. Standing-wave probe ratio (see §D below): R ≈ 0.07

No correction to OUT/IN is needed. The standing wave at 9373 is real but small.

Files: `mansard_funke.py`, `mansard_funke_findings.md`, `mansard_funke.png`

## C. Wind-only coherence analysis (`mf_windonly.py`)

### Motivation

Can Mansard-Funke decomposition characterise the panel's reflection of wind-generated waves
(no paddle), as opposed to paddle waves? This would let us say something about wave
reflection during wind-only conditions — potentially a useful result for the thesis.

### First attempt (failed): broadband MF on wind-only FFT bins

Initial approach: take the FFT of wind-only signals (no paddle), apply Mansard-Funke
per-frequency-bin across 1–8 Hz. Result: R ≈ 1.0 everywhere for all moorings.

**Why this fails (physical reason):** Mansard-Funke requires coherent signals. Wind waves
are broadband and stochastic — at any individual FFT bin, the two probe signals have
essentially random phases relative to each other. For two uncorrelated signals z₁ and z₂
with |z₁| ≈ |z₂|:
  A ∝ (z₁ − z₂) and B ∝ (z₂ − z₁) → |A| ≈ |B| → R → 1 always.
The result (R≈1) is not physical; it is the mathematically inevitable breakdown value for
incoherent input. This is not a code bug — it is a fundamental incompatibility between the
method and the data type.

### Second approach: Welch cross-spectral density coherence

Switched to computing the coherence γ²(f) = |G₁₂|² / (PSD₁·PSD₂) between 8804/250
and 9373/170 for all fullwind nowave runs, concatenated per mooring.

**Result: γ² < 0.2 everywhere (maximum 0.19, median 0.003).**

Even with 1423 s of data concatenated for above_50 and 1616 s for below_90, the coherence
never exceeded 0.37 at any frequency in the 0.5–6 Hz range. The wind-generated wave field
is spatially incoherent at the 569 mm probe separation.

### Physical interpretation

Each probe responds primarily to local turbulent pressure fluctuations rather than to a
coherent wave travelling from one probe to the other. At 569 mm separation, there is no
coherent wave train connecting the two probes at any wind-wave frequency. The two-probe
analysis framework simply does not apply to wind-only waves at this scale.

The ratio |H(f)| = sqrt(PSD₂/PSD₁) ≈ 1.2–1.7 (9373 > 8804) is not a reflection
signature — it is fetch-limited wind-wave growth. Probe 9373/170 is 0.6 m further
downwind (closer to the panel) and sees ~17–18% more wind-wave energy:
  above_50: std(8804)=3.91 mm, std(9373)=4.61 mm (ratio 1.18)
  below_90: std(8804)=3.66 mm, std(9373)=4.29 mm (ratio 1.17)

### What the "visible standing wave" under full wind + above_50 was

The user had previously observed a visible standing-wave pattern under above_50 + full wind
(no paddle). After this analysis it is clear that the wind-only field is spatially incoherent
and cannot produce a standing-wave pattern. The observed pattern must have occurred during
combined wind+paddle runs. The paddle wave is coherent at a single frequency; it reflects
off the panel; the stiffer above-water mooring (short 7–9 cm line) holds the panel in a
fixed position very close to the probe, so the panel's reflected wave can be visible as
a standing-wave node/antinode pattern near the panel. The pattern was at the paddle
frequency, not at a wind-wave frequency.

### Conclusion

Wind-only reflection cannot be measured by two-probe methods at this probe separation.
The panel's reflection coefficient R is characterised from paddle-wave runs only. This is
a methodological limit of the experiment, not a failure of the analysis.

## D. Standing-wave probe ratio fingerprint (`sw_probe_ratio.py`)

### Motivation

User insight: if the standing wave visible at 9373 doesn't reach 8804, then 8804/250 is
a clean far-field reference — purely incident wave, no standing-wave modulation. Then the
ratio |z(9373)/z(8804)| at the paddle frequency directly measures the standing-wave
amplitude factor at x=9.373 m. This is structurally different from Mansard-Funke — it
does not decompose the wave field but simply uses the upstream probe as a known reference.

User confirmed: the standing wave he observed visually at the 9373 probe did not extend
to the 8804 probe location. 9373/170 is ≈1.6 m from the panel (well within the potential
standing-wave zone); 8804/250 is ≈2.2 m from the panel (outside it).

### Method

For each paddle-wave run, compute |z₂/z₁| = |FFT(9373)| / |FFT(8804)| using the same
analysis window for both probes (intersection of both probes' stable-wave windows).

The baseline offset between the two probes — a flat multiplier from calibration differences
and ~0.6 m of additional wind-fetch growth at 9373 — does not oscillate with frequency. It
shifts all frequencies equally. The standing-wave fingerprint appears as a
frequency-dependent oscillation ON TOP of this flat baseline:

  If |z₂/z₁| is flat: no standing wave at 9373 (pure incident at both probes)
  If |z₂/z₁| oscillates with frequency: standing wave at 9373 is real
  Oscillation half-range ≈ R   (from the identity SW_factor ≈ 1 ± R for small R)

### Results (0.2V, nowind)

```
below_90_loose230:   range 0.924–1.074, half-range = 0.075 → R ≈ 0.07–0.08
above_50:            range 0.908–1.054, half-range = 0.073 → R ≈ 0.07
```

The ratio oscillates clearly — it is NOT flat. This is a third independent estimate of R,
fully consistent with the smoothness test bound and the Mansard-Funke result.

The oscillation AMPLITUDE (R ≈ 0.07) is robust to panel position uncertainty.
The oscillation PHASE (which specific frequencies are nodes vs antinodes) is NOT —
panel position uncertainty of ±0.3 m shifts the standing-wave pattern by ±2 rad at
1.4 Hz, nearly a full cycle. The node/antinode assignment cannot be determined without
knowing the panel centroid position to ±0.1 m.

### Conclusion

The standing wave at 9373/170 is real and detectable, but only using the coherent paddle
signal as a carrier. Wind-only waves (§C) are too incoherent to reveal it via amplitude
ratios.

Files: `sw_probe_ratio.py`, `sw_probe_ratio_findings.md`, `sw_probe_ratio.png`

## E. Mooring standing-wave bias: above_50 vs below_90 geometry (`mooring_sw_bias.py`)

### What triggered this

During analysis of §D the user revealed a critical geometric difference between the two
mooring types that had not previously been quantified:

- **above_50**: line ≈ 7–9 cm, attached above water at +50 mm. Panel leading edge sits
  only **≈5 cm** from the 9373/170 IN probe. The short, stiff line gives the panel very
  little longitudinal slack.

- **below_90**: line ≈ 230–300 mm, attached below water at −90 mm. Panel leading edge sits
  **≈20–25 cm** from the 9373/170 IN probe. The longer elastic line allows the panel to
  equilibrate further upstream.

This is not a small difference in experimental conditions — it is a systematic difference
in where the panel sits relative to the IN probe, which changes which part of the
standing-wave pattern the IN probe is sampling.

### The standing-wave amplitude factor and what it does to OUT/IN

With a reflection coefficient R at the panel, the surface elevation at any point x is a
superposition of the incident and reflected waves. The amplitude at position x is modulated
by the standing-wave amplitude factor:

  SW_factor(x, f) = sqrt(1 + R² + 2R·cos(2k·(x_panel − x)))

- At the panel surface (x = x_panel): cos argument = 0 → SW_factor is maximum (antinode
  always at the panel face, as required by the boundary condition)
- One quarter wavelength away: cos argument = π → SW_factor is minimum (node)
- The pattern repeats with spatial period λ/2 = π/k

The IN probe measures this modulated amplitude, not the pure incident amplitude. So:

  OUT/IN_measured = A_out / (A_incident × SW_factor) = T_true / SW_factor

- SW_factor > 1 (near antinode): OUT/IN is DEFLATED — appears lower than true transmission
- SW_factor < 1 (near node): OUT/IN is INFLATED — appears higher than true transmission

### Predicted bias between above_50 and below_90

For above_50 with Δ_panel ≈ 5 cm: the argument 2k·Δ ≈ 0.1k remains small for all
frequencies. cos(2kΔ) ≈ 1 → SW_factor ≈ 1.07 at all frequencies. The IN probe is
permanently near an antinode.

For below_90 with Δ_panel ≈ 25 cm: 2k·Δ ranges from ≈1.4 rad (at 0.8 Hz) to ≈5.8 rad
(at 1.7 Hz). This traverses from a partial antinode through a deep node and back. At
0.9–1.5 Hz, the probe is near a node → SW_factor ≈ 0.93–0.95.

**Net predicted effect on measured OUT/IN**:
- above_50: IN probe overreads by ~7% → measured OUT/IN is deflated by ~7%
- below_90 (0.9–1.5 Hz): IN probe underreads by ~5-12% → measured OUT/IN is inflated

Predicted ratio of measured OUT/IN (above_50 / below_90) = SW_below / SW_above:

```
freq    SW_above50   SW_below90   pred ratio   above_50 appears
0.80    1.068        1.015        0.951        -4.9% lower
0.90    1.066        0.994        0.933        -6.8% lower
1.00    1.065        0.970        0.912        -8.9% lower
1.10    1.062        0.947        0.892        -10.8% lower
1.20    1.059        0.932        0.880        -12.0% lower
1.30    1.055        0.933        0.882        -11.6% lower
1.40    1.051        0.953        0.903        -9.3% lower
1.50    1.045        0.990        0.941        -5.3% lower
1.60    1.038        1.032        0.987        -0.6% lower
1.70    1.030        1.063        1.026        +3.2% higher
```

### Misstep: expecting the data to confirm the theory

The analysis was set up correctly: compute the observed above_50/below_90 OUT/IN ratio
per frequency, then compare to the theoretical SW_below/SW_above. If observed ≈ predicted
→ the observed mooring difference is purely a standing-wave measurement artefact (both
moorings have identical true transmission, just measured with different IN probe biases).

But the data gave: observed ratio ≈ 1.00–1.02 at 0.8–1.4 Hz (above_50 roughly equal
to or slightly higher than below_90), while theory predicts 0.88–0.95 (above_50 should
be noticeably lower). Residuals were +0.06 to +0.12 — systematically in the opposite
direction from prediction.

**The diagnosis**: the analysis is underpowered, not wrong. above_50 has only n=1-4 runs
per frequency at nowind + 0.2V + fullpanel + quality_flag==ok. A single OUT/IN measurement
can vary by ±5% from run to run due to residual sidewall reflections, incomplete stillwater
settling between runs, and signal noise. The theoretical standing-wave effect being tested
is 5-12% — it is entirely within the noise floor at n=1.

**Checking why above_50 has so few nowind runs**: The dataset has 344 rows with
Mooring=above_50, but only 20 pass the nowind + 0.2V + fullpanel + ok filter at ≥0.8 Hz.
The 344 rows are predominantly fullwind runs — the above_50 mooring configuration was used
primarily for the wind experiments (it is the "above-water stiff mooring" case that was
expected to show interesting wind effects). Nowind calibration sweeps were done sparingly,
with typically only n=2 per frequency.

One exception: 1.3 Hz has n=13 at nowind + 0.1V for above_50. This was a dedicated repeat
session investigating the 1.3 Hz standing-wave anomaly (OUT/IN > 1 had been observed), not
a calibration sweep.

### Conclusion

The theoretical standing-wave measurement bias between above_50 and below_90 is real and
quantifiable (5-12% at 0.9–1.5 Hz based on the geometric difference and R≈0.07). But the
dataset does not have enough above_50 nowind runs to confirm or refute it empirically.
The analysis is sound — the data is simply underpowered for this test.

**Practical implications for the thesis**:
- Do NOT compare absolute OUT/IN between mooring types without acknowledging this caveat
- The wind effect WITHIN each mooring type (fullwind vs nowind at fixed mooring) is clean:
  the panel position does not change between wind conditions → the standing-wave bias
  cancels out when computing the wind delta. This is the primary analysis.
- Report mooring types separately; do not pool above_50 and below_90 for absolute OUT/IN

**Recommended thesis note**: "The above_50 mooring configuration (7–9 cm line attached
above water at +50 mm) holds the panel leading edge approximately 5 cm from the IN probe,
placing it permanently near a standing-wave antinode. The below_90 configurations (230–300 mm
lines attached below water at −90 mm) place the panel approximately 25 cm from the IN
probe, which cycles through nodes and antinodes with increasing frequency. With R≈0.07,
this geometric difference creates a predicted 5-12% systematic offset in measured OUT/IN
between the mooring types at 0.9–1.5 Hz. Insufficient nowind data exists to confirm this
offset empirically (n=1–4 per frequency for above_50). Cross-mooring absolute comparisons
are therefore not attempted; each mooring type is analysed independently for the wind
effect."

## Key rules established this session (CRITICAL for future agents)

### 1. Python FFT sign convention in Mansard-Funke

Python `np.fft.fft` positive-freq bin of a rightward wave η = A·cos(kx − ωt):
    z ∝ A · exp(−ikx)    (NOT +ikx)

The linear system to solve:
    z₁ = A · exp(−ikx₁) + B · exp(+ikx₁)
    z₂ = A · exp(−ikx₂) + B · exp(+ikx₂)

Solution (use THIS in code, not the textbook +ikx version):
    A_inc = (z₁·exp(+ikx₂) − z₂·exp(+ikx₁)) / (2i·sin(kΔ))
    B_ref = (z₂·exp(−ikx₁) − z₁·exp(−ikx₂)) / (2i·sin(kΔ))

Diagnostic: for a pure incident wave, arg(z₂/z₁) should equal −kΔ (mod 2π).

### 2. Mansard-Funke requires coherent signals

Applying Mansard-Funke to wind-only (incoherent stochastic) signals always gives R → 1
regardless of physics. This is not a code failure — it is a mathematical necessity.
Use Mansard-Funke only for runs with a coherent single-frequency paddle signal.

### 3. Cross-mooring absolute comparison requires standing-wave bias caveat

The above_50 panel is always near the standing-wave antinode at the IN probe (SW_factor≈1.07);
below_90 cycles through nodes and antinodes across frequencies (SW_factor 0.93–1.07).
A 5-12% systematic offset in measured OUT/IN results between the two mooring types.
The wind effect WITHIN each mooring type is unaffected — the bias cancels in the delta.
